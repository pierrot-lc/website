---
title: Craftsmanship
---
Like everyone else, I am starting to get tired of AI news. I used to overlook the criticism but now
I am more and more sick of hearing big AI labs claim that yet another incredible math problem has
fallen. I feel weird whenever I see AI-generated stuff. Do people actually like what they generate?
Would they consume it? This is particularly true for blogposts. I spend maybe an hour every day
scanning the web for opinions and interesting news, and discovering something is AI-generated is so
frustrating. And most of the time, it feels like they didn't even read their post. Writing online is
sharing your own thoughts. [You should care.][normalize-caring]

While I believe most people agree in the case of blog posts, I belong to the minority who thinks the
same reasoning is applicable to code. Should I be using your code for something important, it should
inspire me trust and care. LLMs are just a tool, use them however you want, but please be mindful
about what you create. I want to feel respected, just as I felt respected the first time I tried
linux compared to windows. If you've already tried linux, you should understand perfectly what I
mean, so why stop here?

I know, the incentive in the industry is different. The goal is to create value in a timely manner.
Yet, you can feel when a product is designed by a loving team that had the end-user experience in
mind from the start. That's why I love brands like [ZSA][zsa] and [framework][framework]. Yes those
products are expensive, but that's the price you have to pay for a development that was afforded
time and respected its workers.

I've met developers who told me they never read the code anymore. My guess is that they cared about
code quality just because they knew they would have to understand and modify it at some point in the
future. Now that they can get away without touching the code, they don't care at all. Is that how
someone becomes a manager? To be fair, in the real world you have pressure to deliver, and even if
you like your craft it can be very hard to resist taking the shortest path.

Some other developers truly are tinkerers and they only care about the end result. AI is a
superpower to them. That's all understandable, but such projects should remain personal for the most
part. And I am not saying computers shouldn't be fun! Experiment and use AI in your own way! I would
just like people to understand the difference between a guy brainlessly one-shotting something and
the other carefully making it. We are already surrounded by AI-generated content; it's important to
consider how something came to be.

You should care about the details, even the ones that are invisible to others. Love your craft,
think hard about the problem, decompose it into simple pieces, make your code easy to read and
understand, and master every part of it.

I fucking love to train my AIs and to think about what sick ideas I could try for my next project,
but I implement them without using LLMs. I still use AI regularly, I ask for advice, code reviews,
or suggestions, but I always rephrase the output to make it my own.
