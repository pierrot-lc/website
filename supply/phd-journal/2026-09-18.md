Holidays were necessary and Scotland is beautiful. People there are really friendly and can't make a
sentence without inserting a joke. Starting the 3rd year without a paper is stressful and I suspect
it has something to do with my back hurting me so bad lately.

So, *Yet Another PENS Rejection*, the 4th! My supervisors were supportive, more than usual. I told
them about my struggles and they are receptive. I also talked to my friends and other PhD students
and the fact that I am not the only one feeling like that is somehow reassuring.

With these holidays, my back hurting, and the other people in the same position as me made me
realize: *it's not that bad!* I am pretty proud about my research so far, and even if I don't make
it into an impressive carrier, I'll be fine.

---

As everyone else, I am starting to get tired about AI news. I used to overlook the criticism but now
I am more and more sick of hearing big AI labs claiming that yet another incredible math problem has
fallen. Whenever I see AI generated stuff I feel weird. Do people actually like what they generate?
Would they consume it? This is particularly true for blogposts. I spend maybe 1 hour everyday
scanning the web for opinions and interesting news. Discovering something is AI generated is so much
disrespectful. And most of the time, it feels like they didn't even read their post. If the goal is
to look like your blog is alive, I pass. Writing online is sharing your own thoughts. You should
care.

While I believe most people agree for the case of blogposts, I belong to the minority who thinks the
same reasoning is applicable to code. The incentive here is different. The goal of a software
developer is to create value, and in a timely manner. Yet, if I have to spend my time using your
product, I want to do so on a product that has been created with passion. You can feel when a
product is designed by a loving team that had the end user experience in mind from the start. That's
why I love brands like [ZSA](https://www.zsa.io/backpack) and [framework](https://frame.work/) for
example. Yes those products are expensive, but that's the price you have to pay for a development
that afforded time and respected their workers.

I meet developers who told me they never ever read the code now. My guess is that they cared about
code quality just because they knew they would have to understand and modify it at some point in the
future. Now that they can get away without touching the code, they don't care at all. Is that how
someone becomes a manager? To be fair, in the real world you have the pressure to deliver and even
if you like your craft it can be very hard to fight against Codex. Being out of the industry I am
thankfully free from such pressure (still I've heard that even in academia you can be pushed to use
such tools!).

I fucking love to train my AIs and to think about what sick ideas I could try for my next project,
but I implement them without LLMs.
